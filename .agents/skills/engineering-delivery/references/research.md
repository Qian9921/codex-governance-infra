# Research and numerical work

Use this card when a task makes a scientific, numerical, benchmark, or research claim.

- State the question and comparison before choosing an experiment.
- Separate observed results, interpretation, and planned work. Do not turn a passing example into a framework-wide claim.
- Record the exact source revision, data/configuration, command, and metric needed to reproduce the result; use an existing repository record instead of adding a tracking system.
- Define the evaluation population and denominator before selecting examples. Preserve failures, pathological cases, and unobserved cases.
- Compare against an independent reference when judging replacement or numerical parity; mutual agreement is not proof.
- Use tolerances and error metrics that match the quantity, conditioning, units, and failure risk.
- Stop at the requested evidence boundary. Do not create a baseline, hash gate, or contract merely because it sounds rigorous.

Keep conclusions scoped to the tested family and report uncertainty honestly. Consult [NASA SPD-41a](https://science.nasa.gov/science-red/s3fs-public/atoms/files/SMD-information-policy-SPD-41a.pdf) for provenance and reproducibility context when relevant.
